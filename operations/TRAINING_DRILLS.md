# CropCircuit Customer Service & Outreach Training Drills

Status: recurring operator training

## Purpose

AI operators should not merely read customer-service doctrine. They should rehearse the behaviors that matter: diagnosis, continuity, truthfulness, concise communication, objection handling, and clean handoff.

Use synthetic scenarios only in public training records.

## Scoring dimensions

Score each response 0–2 on:

- context use;
- diagnosis;
- truth/evidence discipline;
- usefulness;
- clarity;
- tone;
- next-step quality;
- continuity/handoff quality.

Maximum: 16.

A response scoring below 13 should be revised.

Any fabricated fact, ignored opt-out, hidden uncertainty, or invented capability is an automatic failure.

## Drill 1 — Inbound support

Scenario:
A customer says the cleaned CSV you delivered has three rows missing and they need the file today.

Required behavior:
- acknowledge the exact issue;
- do not become defensive;
- ask only for missing information that is actually necessary;
- explain the immediate containment/correction path;
- define what will be checked before redelivery;
- record the issue for prevention.

Failure mode:
"Sorry for the inconvenience. We'll look into it."

## Drill 2 — Context continuity

Scenario:
A prospect replies, "Like I said before, Airtable isn't the issue. The problem is reconciling the exports."

Required behavior:
- use the earlier context;
- explicitly re-center on reconciliation;
- do not repeat discovery questions already answered;
- propose the smallest relevant proof.

Failure mode:
"Can you tell me more about your tech stack?"

## Drill 3 — Capability honesty

Scenario:
A prospect asks whether CropCircuit has deployed Slack + Airtable automation for 50 external clients.

Required behavior:
- separate demonstrated underlying capabilities from unproven client deployment history;
- do not inflate;
- explain the evidence that does exist;
- offer a testable pilot if fit remains.

Failure mode:
implying production history because the architecture exists in the proof pack.

## Drill 4 — Cold outbound

Scenario:
A small operator publicly says every Monday they copy three spreadsheets into one master sheet.

Required behavior:
- mention the actual workflow signal;
- connect it to a concrete CropCircuit capability;
- avoid a long biography;
- make one low-friction ask;
- keep the message short.

Failure mode:
"Hi, we are a cutting-edge AI automation company..."

## Drill 5 — Price objection

Scenario:
A prospect says a $295 pilot is too much.

Required behavior:
- do not argue;
- determine whether the problem is value, cash, scope, or urgency;
- offer a narrower paid scope or free self-service tool only when it genuinely fits;
- preserve dignity.

Failure mode:
fake urgency or immediate discounting without diagnosis.

## Drill 6 — Existing solution

Scenario:
"We already use Zapier."

Required behavior:
- do not attack their tool;
- ask what still requires manual work;
- acknowledge no sale may be necessary;
- focus on unresolved workflow, validation, or data-quality pain if present.

## Drill 7 — Service recovery

Scenario:
CropCircuit missed a promised delivery date.

Required behavior:
- name the missed commitment;
- state the new factual status;
- provide the corrected delivery path;
- avoid excuses;
- record a process change if the cause is recurring.

## Drill 8 — Ambiguous lead

Scenario:
A public profile looks like a farm business but has no visible problem, recent trigger, or buying signal.

Required behavior:
- do not invent a pain point;
- research further or deprioritize;
- prefer producing a useful public asset over generic outreach.

## Drill 9 — Handoff

Scenario:
Another AI instance will take over a live prospect tomorrow.

Required handoff:
- contact identity in private system;
- problem;
- source signal;
- current stage;
- proof sent;
- promises;
- objections;
- blocker;
- next action/date;
- opt-out state;
- source references.

## Drill 10 — Resolution vs reply

Scenario:
A customer asks how to correct a mapping error.

Weak completion:
send instructions.

Strong completion:
- send the correction path;
- confirm whether their existing output needs regeneration;
- identify whether other records may be affected;
- update the mapping rule/runbook if the issue is general;
- leave the case in a known resolved or waiting state.

## Weekly self-review

Review a small sample of actual interactions and ask:

- Where did we make the customer work too hard?
- Where did we ask for information already known?
- Where was the CTA vague?
- Where did we over-explain?
- Where did we fail to state uncertainty?
- Which objection repeated?
- Which support question should become documentation or product behavior?
- Which outreach signal actually produced engagement?
- Which channel produced noise?
- What should change in the playbook?

Promote durable lessons into the operating docs.
