# CropCircuit Customer Service & Outreach Playbook

Status: active  
Audience: CropCircuit operators and agents

## Core principle

**Useful before persuasive. Personal before automated. Resolution before volume.**

Customer service and outreach are one system: every interaction should increase clarity, trust, and the probability that the right customer gets the right outcome.

## 1. Customer service standard

### First response

Respond as quickly as practical. For email, same-business-day is the default target for straightforward messages; acknowledge sooner when the issue is urgent or blocks a customer.

If the full solution is not ready, do not disappear. A useful acknowledgment should say:
- what we understood;
- what we can do now;
- what remains;
- what happens next.

### Resolution ownership

The conversation is not complete because we replied. It is complete when:
- the question is answered;
- the deliverable is accepted or clearly handed off;
- the next responsible party is known;
- unresolved risk is visible.

### Context continuity

Before replying:
1. read the thread;
2. inspect relevant prior work;
3. use the customer's actual terminology;
4. do not ask for information already supplied.

Maintain one coherent story across sales, fulfillment, and support.

### Personalization

Personalization means relevance, not token replacement.

Use:
- the customer's stated goal;
- their actual workflow;
- their recent interaction;
- a concrete constraint;
- a specific artifact or signal.

Do not manufacture personal details or pretend familiarity.

### Tone

Default:
- human;
- calm;
- competent;
- concise;
- non-defensive.

When something went wrong:
1. acknowledge the specific impact;
2. state what is known;
3. correct it when possible;
4. explain the next step;
5. note how recurrence will be reduced when relevant.

Do not blame the customer.

## 2. Outreach standard

### Qualification before contact

Do not contact a prospect merely because an email address exists.

At minimum identify:
- who they are;
- why they might plausibly have the problem;
- what signal makes the timing relevant;
- which CropCircuit capability maps to that problem;
- whether there is evidence they can buy or act.

Preferred signals:
- explicit request for help;
- job post / RFP / funded project;
- public operational pain;
- new program or workflow;
- recent growth/change that creates the problem;
- existing engagement with CropCircuit.

### Message architecture

A good first message usually has four parts:

1. **Context** — one specific reason this person is being contacted.
2. **Problem hypothesis** — the friction we believe may exist, phrased tentatively.
3. **Proof/value** — one relevant capability, sample, or concrete outcome.
4. **Small ask** — an easy next step.

Do not open with a long company biography.

### Evidence over adjectives

Prefer:
- "We built a mapper that normalizes inconsistent CSV columns into an explicit schema."

Over:
- "We are a cutting-edge AI automation company."

Proof links, examples, and narrow pilots reduce trust friction.

### Low-friction offers

Prefer a small proof before a large engagement.

Examples:
- one sample file;
- one workflow;
- one operational review;
- one fixed-scope pilot;
- one paid diagnostic that produces a useful artifact.

A pilot should have:
- price;
- boundary;
- deliverables;
- timing;
- exclusions;
- success criteria.

### Follow-up cadence

Internal default for qualified outbound unless channel/context suggests otherwise:

- Touch 1: relevant introduction + concrete value.
- Touch 2: 2–3 business days later; add one useful detail, example, or clarification.
- Touch 3: about 4–5 business days later; concise restatement tied to their problem.
- Touch 4: close-the-loop message; make it easy to decline or revisit later.

Do not send four copies of the same message.

Stop when:
- they decline;
- they opt out;
- the signal goes stale;
- the fit is disproven;
- channel rules require it.

### Multi-channel behavior

Use multiple channels only when appropriate and respectful.

The message should remain coherent across channels. Do not make a prospect feel surrounded by simultaneous automated contacts.

## 3. Discovery

The goal of discovery is diagnosis, not persuasion.

Learn:
- current workflow;
- desired outcome;
- frequency/volume;
- current manual cost or pain;
- systems involved;
- failure modes;
- sensitivity/privacy requirements;
- who decides;
- deadline;
- what "done" means.

Do not sell a complex integration before understanding the workflow.

## 4. Objections

Treat objections as information.

### "Too expensive"
Clarify the comparison:
- cost of manual work;
- cost of error/delay;
- whether the scope is too broad;
- whether a smaller pilot is useful.

Do not pressure.

### "Need to think"
Ask what information is missing. Provide it. Set a reasonable follow-up point.

### "We already have a tool"
Ask what remains painful. If the existing system solves the problem, say so and do not force a sale.

### "Can you do X?"
Answer in three buckets:
- demonstrated now;
- feasible but needs scoping/testing;
- outside current capability.

Never collapse these categories.

## 5. Handoff from sale to fulfillment

Before work begins, record:

- customer;
- paid status;
- exact scope;
- input files/systems;
- exclusions;
- due target;
- acceptance criteria;
- sensitive-data constraints;
- promised revisions;
- communication channel.

No fulfillment should depend on reconstructing a sales conversation from memory.

## 6. Service recovery

When CropCircuit makes an error:

1. identify the exact error;
2. contain impact;
3. correct deliverable/data/process;
4. tell the customer what changed;
5. add a prevention step when justified.

Never quietly patch customer-facing data without noting a material correction.

## 7. Metrics that matter

Track:
- first response time;
- time to resolution;
- qualified conversations;
- positive reply rate;
- pilot/meeting conversion;
- paid conversion;
- delivery on-time rate;
- revision/rework rate;
- refund rate;
- repeat purchase/referral;
- common objections;
- top reasons for no-fit.

Do not optimize raw send volume as the primary metric.

## 8. CRM / contact record minimum

Every real prospect/customer record should contain:

- person + organization;
- source/signal;
- problem hypothesis;
- relevant offer;
- evidence/proof sent;
- last contact date;
- response;
- status;
- next action + date;
- opt-out/do-not-contact state;
- notes needed for continuity.

## 9. Anti-spam rules

Never:
- scrape and blast indiscriminately;
- fake prior contact;
- pretend a manual review happened when it did not;
- hide that a message is commercial when disclosure is required;
- send deceptive reply-thread subjects;
- keep contacting someone who declined;
- use private/sensitive personal data for personalization.

## 10. CropCircuit-specific selling posture

CropCircuit's strongest sales posture is:

**We found a real operational problem, built a concrete artifact around it, and can implement the same pattern for you within a clear boundary.**

Lead with:
- working public tools;
- schemas/contracts;
- validation;
- local-first workflows;
- transparent assumptions;
- synthetic case studies;
- fixed-scope implementation.

Avoid selling a giant abstract transformation project before a smaller proof works.

## Research basis

This playbook is informed by current 2026 guidance emphasizing:
- fast first response and reduced resolution time;
- personalized, human communication;
- preservation of customer context;
- self-service for repeated questions;
- research-driven, signal-based prospecting;
- avoiding generic batch-and-blast outreach;
- using AI to reduce research/admin work while keeping human relevance.
