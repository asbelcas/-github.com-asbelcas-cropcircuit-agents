# CropCircuit Outreach Qualification Gate

Status: active

## Goal

Protect attention and brand trust by contacting fewer, better prospects.

A contact should normally pass the gate before outbound outreach.

## Gate

## Gate 0 — Suppression check

Before researching fit or drafting any message, check the private do-not-contact/suppression state and relevant thread history.

If the contact has explicitly opted out:
- stop;
- do not qualify them for a new offer;
- do not treat a new campaign as permission;
- do not draft a "last try" or "different offer" message.

A prior opt-out overrides every downstream qualification signal unless the person later re-initiates contact and clearly asks to communicate.


### 1. Problem fit

Can CropCircuit name a plausible operational problem this person or organization may actually have?

Pass only when supported by a real signal or clear role/workflow evidence.

### 2. Timing signal

Is there a reason the conversation makes sense now?

Examples:
- explicit request;
- job/RFP;
- public workflow complaint;
- new program launch;
- staffing/growth change;
- active engagement with CropCircuit;
- recent tool adoption or migration;
- repeated manual process publicly described.

Generic industry membership is weak timing evidence.

### 3. Capability fit

Can CropCircuit point to something demonstrated that maps to the problem?

Use:
- working tool;
- schema;
- validator;
- synthetic case study;
- relevant fixed-scope service;
- code already in the repo.

If capability is only plausible but unproven, say so and offer a pilot.

### 4. Buyer path

Is there a plausible person, budget, or decision path?

Strong:
- funded job/RFP;
- decision-maker;
- business owner;
- department owner;
- existing customer.

Weak:
- anonymous account;
- no authority;
- no evidence of budget;
- speculative public profile.

### 5. Contact legitimacy

Is the channel appropriate and the contact information obtained legitimately?

Do not use sensitive/private personal information for targeting.

### 6. Message value

Can the first message contain useful information, proof, or a concrete hypothesis rather than merely asking for time/money?

### 7. Small next step

Can we ask for something low-friction?

Examples:
- confirm whether the workflow exists;
- send a non-sensitive sample;
- review a short proof page;
- answer one scoping question;
- choose whether a pilot is worth discussing.

## Signal hierarchy

Prefer, in order:

1. inbound request / active buying intent;
2. funded job or explicit project;
3. existing relationship with new need;
4. recent, specific operational trigger;
5. repeated engagement with relevant CropCircuit content;
6. strong ICP fit without timing signal;
7. broad list membership.

Spend most attention near the top.

## Disqualifiers

Do not pursue when:
- they declined;
- no plausible fit exists;
- the only strategy is generic mass email;
- project funding is unverifiable and custom work would be required up front;
- the ask would require misrepresenting experience;
- channel rules prohibit solicitation;
- expected value is lower than the opportunity cost of serving existing qualified work.

## Pre-send note

For every meaningful outbound message, be able to complete this sentence:

> "I am contacting this person now because ______, and the specific useful thing CropCircuit can offer is ______."

If that sentence is generic, research more or do not send.
